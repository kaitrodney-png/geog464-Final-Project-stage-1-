About This Map

Welcome to the Woodland Caribou Québec mapping project. This website explores the current state of key Caribou habitats throughout Quebec. Caribou are an endagered species protected by the Candian species at risk act, as their populations have seen massive amounts of decline in recent years. Quebec is lucky enough home to 3 different subpopulations ofCaribou. That are each unique in their own way. The Gaspésie population is the most endangered of the three, with only 76 individuals left in the wild. The Forestier population is the largest of the three, with an estimated 3,000 individuals. The Montagnard population is the least endangered of the three, with an estimated 1,000 individuals. Although all 3 subpopulations are unique, they are all facing a similar threat, this is loss or critical habitat. The goal of this map is to provide a visual representation of the different Anthropogenic and natural disturbances that are affecting the Caribou populaations today.

How to use this map

While exploring the map you can first view the Caribou heards at a provincial level, in regards to anthropogenic disturbances. This provides a general overvieww of the current state of their habitats. You can then pick individual herds to view their specific situation, once on their view you can then also so natural diturbances that have happened in the area along with the main linear disturbances such as roads, powerlines, ect. You also get a more detailed overview of the populations current status.

Key information about this page

Each individual heard of Caribou, are given their own stability level. This refers to how vulnerable each group is to disturbances. A stability level of 1 is extremly vulnerbale and a level of 4 is least vulnerbale.

When looking at the anthropogenic disturbances, it is important to identify the key disturbances are broken down into 7 main categories each represented by a different color.

Cutblock: Areas where forest vegetation has been removed through forestry operations.

Settlement: Areas associated with communities, buildings, and other developed land.

Mine: Areas associated with mining and mineral extraction activities.

Agriculture: Areas used for agricultural activities and farming.

Harvest: Areas associated with timber harvesting and forestry activities.

Oil/Gas: Areas associated with oil and gas exploration and extraction.

Unknown: Disturbances for which the specific category has not been identified.

Gaps in the Data

I also want to acknowledge, that although there are 3 subpopulations of Caribou in Qubec, there are still large gaps in the data shared. For the purpose of this map the Montagnard population, is not identified at all as their geographical distribution is not well documented. The Gaspésie population although it is identified, has limited data available. Due to the various gaps in the research the data about the varying disturbances were not able to be represetned on this map Lastly is the Forest caribou, they are the main focus of the map as they are currently the most documented and reseached subpopulation. This means that there are still many unknowns about the current state of Caribou populations in Quebec. Hopefully this map can help identify some of these gaps.
